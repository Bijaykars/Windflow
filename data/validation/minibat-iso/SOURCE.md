## Data issue: measured exit velocity vs the tabulated U0

The hot-wire field does not match U0 = 1.67 m/s (68 m3/h through D = 0.12 m). On T1, 3 cm past the exit, the jet core
reads about 3.0 m/s over a region about 0.12 m wide (max |U| = 3.05 m/s). Summed over T1's 2 x 2 cm cells (masked cells
= 0), the plane carries 0.0306 m3/s (110 m3/h) and a momentum flux of 0.0718 m4/s2, **2.3x** the tabulated
U0^2 pi D^2/4 = 0.0315. The momentum flux is nearly conserved downstream (T2 0.068, T3 0.060, T4 0.054), as it should be
in a free jet, so the hot-wire data are self-consistent. K08 Fig. 6a (p.78) normalises the maximum velocity by U_in with
U_M/U_in = 1 at y = 0.6 m and 0.45 at y = 1.5 m; the dataset's M1 maxima are 3.02 m/s at y = 0.6 m and 1.25 m/s at
1.5 m (ratio 0.41), so K08's U_in is the measured exit velocity (about 3 m/s), not 1.67 m/s. K08 p.77 also imposed
"known values using the experimental data" at the inlet of its CFD. We therefore drive the supply with the **measured T1
momentum flux** by default (`--inlet measured`); `--inlet table` runs the tabulated 1.67 m/s for comparison. The
compared quantity is |U| divided by the tabulated U0 = 1.67 m/s throughout (normalisation does not change FAC2, FB or
NMSE).

## How the numbers in `measured.csv` were obtained

By script (`convert.py`, openpyxl) from the five isothermal `Airflow_dynamics.xlsx` files, not digitised. Each sheet has
a title row, two header rows (X Y Z Vx Vy Vz txx tyy tzz txy txz tyz and their units) and one row per position; every
numeric cell is written with Python `repr`, and the script reads `measured.csv` back and asserts all 2463 x 12 values
are identical to the spreadsheet. One T2 row holds only an x value and is dropped. Columns:

- `vx_mps`, `vy_mps`, `vz_mps`: mean velocity components. `txx` ... `tyz`: Reynolds-stress components (m2/s2). These
  are **turbulence, not measurement uncertainty**; the sheets have no rms-of-the-mean or uncertainty column. The only
  published uncertainty is 0.05 m/s on the mean velocity (DiB p.5), which the plots use as the bar and the metrics as W.
- `status` (our label): `ok` = measured |U| >= 0.1 m/s (1419 positions, scored); `below0.1` = non-zero but below the
  probe's stated 0.1 m/s floor (515, kept, not scored); `masked` = all nine quantities exactly 0 (529, not scored). The
  sources do not say what an all-zero row means; they cluster outside the jet (282 of T1's 396), which fits positions
  that were not measured or were rejected. Per plane (ok / below0.1 / masked): M1 682/144/54, T1 42/72/282,
  T2 154/110/131, T3 240/106/50, T4 301/83/12.

## How WindFlow runs it (see `src/WindFlow.Validation/Minibat.cs`)

- Full scale. `RunOptions.MinDx` lowers the 0.1 m cell floor so the room runs at Dx = 0.1/N m with the nozzle N cells
  across (default N = 4, Dx = 25 mm, 1.85 M cells; `--nozzle-cells 5|6` for 20 / 16.7 mm). The harness Runner passes
  MinDx to `Voxelizer.Build` the same way `Simulation.Run` does.
- Walls/ceiling are 2-cell boxes; the housing is one slab per cell layer (the rasteriser marks faces only); the
  extraction box is a 1-cell lid and two 1-cell side walls over its 0.20 x 0.10 m opening, drained by an open `Opening`
  through the wall into a 0.3 m plenum open at +y (zero wind). The round nozzle is a square `Patch` supply, N x N cells
  = 0.10 x 0.10 m (0.88 of the round area) in the first air cell in front of the housing, momentum flux matched.
- Compared: |U|/U0 at the 1419 `ok` positions on all five planes, each plane one line; FAC2 / FB / NMSE / q with
  W = 0.05 m/s. Diagnostics in metrics.json: delivered/set flow (extract throat, room planes y = 1.0/2.0/2.7 m, the jet
  one cell past the patch) and the jet-maximum decay on M1 against the measured maxima.

## MINIBAT inlet: disk option (`--inlet disk-table | disk-momentum`, task F2, diagnostic, NOT the default)

**What the real diffuser looks like.** Neither paper dimensions it beyond the D = 0.12 m exit (Table above). DiB
Fig. 2 ("Air supplier system") is a rendered drawing, not machine-readable text (its labels did not survive
`pypdf` extraction, checked against `raw/gresse2020-dib.pdf.txt` in this directory); reading the render directly
gives a nozzle of phi 0.12 m x 0.08 m long, preceded by a phi 0.24 m / 0.20 m chamber and a 0.10 m cone (recorded
in the geometry table above). K08 Fig. 2 is the same drawing, labelled "Diameter 0.12 m"; its text adds only that
"The inlet diffuser has been designed to create an axisymmetric air jet in the room. For the three cases tested,
the measurements show flat velocity profiles at the inlet" (K08 p.77, `raw/kuznik2008.pdf.txt` line 251) -- i.e. a
flat (top-hat) exit profile is the design intent, not a fan-like or swirling one. A later paper by the same group,
Gresse, Merlier & Kuznik (2024), *Building and Environment* 253, describes the physical hardware as "an air
diffuser of complex design" (as relayed in the task brief for F2; that 2024 paper was not fetched for this task --
no PDF is in `raw/`, so no page reference is given here). That is consistent with the chamber+cone reading above
(a settling chamber and a contraction cone ahead of the round exit, not a plain hole in the wall) and is why
WindFlow's own supply approximation -- whether the default square patch or this disk option -- only reproduces
the round EXIT geometry and a uniform/matched exit condition, not the internal diffuser hardware.
**The `--inlet disk` option.** Adds a staircase disk of diameter D = 0.12 m (the true nozzle diameter, not the
default's area-kept 0.10 x 0.10 m square) to the grid: `Minibat.cs` builds a candidate 0.12 x 0.12 m square `Patch`
supply (reusing Core's existing rectangular device geometry, since Core has no round supply-patch primitive and
this task makes no Core changes), then its `InspectGrid` hook trims the corner cells outside radius D/2 from
Fixed back to Air once the grid exists -- a "staircase disk" by construction. `LbmSolver`'s Supply flux controller
(`BuildSupplyLoops`) reads its target from `AirSource.FlowRate` at session start, not from the cell footprint, so
trimming before the solver starts is sufficient; `InspectGrid` also corrects `FlowRate` to the exact target on the
disk's actual (rasterised) area, since a voxelised circle is never exactly pi D^2/4 (recorded per run as
`disk_area_over_analytic` in metrics.json).
Two sub-choices, both diagnostic variants of the disk geometry (documented, per the task; the *default* stays the
0.10 m square, `--inlet measured`, unchanged):
- **`--inlet disk-table`**: matches the tabulated flow, Q = 68 m3/h (K08 Table 1, the flowmeter reading) through
  the disk -- the literal "nameplate" number, spread over the true exit area, so its velocity (about 1.67 m/s,
  U0 itself, since the disk's rasterised area is close to the analytic circle) is far below the ~3.0 m/s the
  hot-wire actually measures 3 cm downstream (see "Data issue" above) -- expected to underpredict the jet.
- **`--inlet disk-momentum`**: matches the measured T1 momentum flux (same quantity and method as the default
  `--inlet measured`, `sum(vy|vy|) dA` over T1's 2 x 2 cm cells) on the disk's actual area instead of the square
  patch's -- the disk is closer to the true nozzle area than the square (0.12 x 0.12 disk vs 0.10 x 0.10 square,
  `nozzle_area_ratio_patch_over_round` in metrics.json shows how close), so its implied exit velocity is closer to
  the true, smaller true-nozzle-area exit velocity than the default square's.
**Not run.** Per the task brief, this option is added and built only (`dotnet build`, `-- selftest`); it is not
exercised with `-- minibat-iso --inlet disk-table` in this task, so its actual FAC2/FB/decay numbers are not in
VALIDATION.md. `Build()` for all four `--inlet` values was exercised directly (no Core/Voxelizer call) in a
scratch harness during development to confirm the candidate geometry and target flow rates are sane before any
grid is built; see this task's report for those numbers.
